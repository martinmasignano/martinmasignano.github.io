There's an image of teaching that has never quite convinced me: one person talks, everyone else listens, and at the end each person leaves with a handful of answers.

I'm not saying there aren't moments to listen to someone who knows more. There are, and they're valuable. But the things we truly learn often need something else: getting our hands involved, asking an awkward question, trying an approach that doesn't work, listening to how someone else sees things.

I spent years working in experience design and leading teams across different disciplines. One of the things I enjoyed most was seeing how a conversation could change the way we approached a problem. Not because someone had the definitive answer, but because a question appeared that we hadn't thought to ask.

That's why I like to think of learning as a shared experience. A space where the person guiding the group brings their experience and tools, while making room for everyone else to bring theirs. Where doing, talking, and revisiting what we've done matter just as much as theory.

I like gatherings that leave us wanting to keep thinking, try something different, or talk to someone we've just met.

Maybe that's what interests me most about teaching: not ending a conversation, but opening up new ones.
