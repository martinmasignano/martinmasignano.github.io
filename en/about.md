# Hi, I'm Martín.

I live in Buenos Aires, and a few years ago I chose to build a simpler life: fewer things, less noise, and more time. Fewer distractions, more books. Fewer demands from others, more walks, more writing, and more meaningful conversations.

## I work with stories

I find them in design, film, and literature. I'm an experience designer, audiovisual editor, and editorial reader. Although these may seem like different disciplines, I see something they have in common: the possibility of giving shape to ideas, connecting people, and finding new ways to look at what's in front of us.

I'm especially interested in what happens along the way. When an idea starts to take shape, when a story finds its structure, when a conversation opens up a possibility we hadn't considered before.

When I'm not working, I'm usually reading, watching a film, or listening to music. I don't draw a sharp line between those things and my work: many ideas arrive precisely when I'm doing something else. Some of those readings find a place in **Libros Vitales**, where I share what I'm discovering.

## Design is also about learning

For many years I worked on digital and service experiences. At Banco Galicia, I led the Service Design team and various Research and Product Design teams, collaborating with people from different disciplines.

Over time I realized that one of the parts of my work I enjoyed most was helping others grow. Sharing knowledge, asking questions, exchanging perspectives, and creating spaces where ideas could be put to the test.

I believe learning is a shared experience. I'm drawn to gatherings where people learn by doing, where there's room to experiment, make mistakes, talk, and build things together.

I don't think teaching is just about passing on what you know. It's also about sparking questions, opening up possibilities, and continuing to learn from the people in front of you.

## What drives me

I enjoy working with curious people who are willing to explore and see things from different angles.

I believe the best experiences are the ones that leave us with new questions, not the ones that give us answers.
